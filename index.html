01 / Featured completed analytics project

Evercrest Services Group

2026 Employee Pulse Study — Evaluating employee experience trends and retention intentions across three survey waves.

Independent portfolio projectSynthetic dataPower BIjamoviR

From workforce questions to organizational recommendations

This end-to-end study examines changes in leadership trust, employee experiences in higher-pressure departments, and workplace factors associated with Intent to Stay. I analyzed a synthetic longitudinal workforce dataset, developed an executive dashboard, and translated the results into practical recommendations.

The project demonstrates survey analysis, nonparametric testing, multiple regression, exploratory factor analysis, statistical interpretation, and executive reporting. Results are illustrative and do not describe a real employer.

Executive dashboard · Page 1

Select the preview to open the full two-page dashboard.

Selected findings

Leadership trust declined

Mean trust fell from 3.35 in Wave 1 to 3.11 in Wave 3; the matched-wave Friedman test was significant (p = .008).

Department group differences

Operations and Customer Services reported lower workload manageability, engagement, and leadership trust than other departments in the grouped comparison.

Retention intentions have multiple correlates

Workload manageability, career growth, and leadership trust were all positively associated with Intent to Stay in the Wave 3 regression (R² = .254).

03 / Independent research · In development

Reluctant retention & career mobility

Exploring why employees remain in jobs they want to leave, what shapes their perceived options, and how those experiences change over time.

Exploratory study · In development

What keeps someone in a job they've already decided they want to leave?

This developing research examines the space between wanting to leave and actually leaving: perceived constraints, familiarity, career transferability, opportunities, and the consequences of staying or moving on.

It considers three perspectives: current reluctant stayers, people who have since left a job they wanted to leave, and satisfied employees as a comparison group.

Qualitative explorationCareer mobilityEmployee experience
Wanting to leave and being able to leave are not necessarily the same experience.
Guiding idea — not a validated study finding

Early lines of inquiry

These are tentative patterns noted during informal exploratory conversations, not established or generalizable findings.

Familiarity and uncertainty

How comfort with known routines and uncertainty about starting over may influence decisions to stay.

Disengagement without departure

How someone may continue performing their role while their emotional investment in it changes.

Perceived mobility

How skills, career identity, external opportunities, and practical constraints shape what leaving feels possible.

Research transparency: This is independent, preliminary exploration based on informal conversations. No formal hypothesis testing, representative sampling, or validated framework is claimed. No participant-level or identifying information is published here.

Where the research could go

Refine the interview guide and conceptual definitions
Compare experiences across current stayers, former stayers, and satisfied employees
Develop a formal study protocol, seek appropriate ethics review, and evaluate quantitative measurement options
